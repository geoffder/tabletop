# Tilted view

From ZÈRTZ, retrofitted onto a finished straight-down board: the board seen from the near side of the table, with pieces standing up from it. All six GIPF project titles (ZÈRTZ, GIPF, LYNGK, YINSH, DVONN, TZAAR) now use it at tilt 0.75.

## Projection

- **One view object owns the projection.** A `BoardView` with a `tilt` ratio (1 = straight down) gives every space's centre (`y × tilt`), every height on screen (`height × √(1 − tilt²)`), the frame and the shape paths. Every centre lookup (rendering, labels, the animator's flight paths, hit areas) goes through it, so the straight-down view is `tilt = 1` of the same code and the switch is a title preference with a toggle.
- **Choosing the angle.** Show a labelled grid of the board at several tilts with pieces on it, then a finer grid between the two the user prefers. ZÈRTZ went 0.5 / 0.65 / 0.8, then 0.65 / 0.7 / 0.75 / 0.8; the user chose 0.75.
- **Circles on the board become ellipses.** A stroked circle inside `scale(1 tilt)` gives an exact flattened ring, strokes included. Outlines are better as `<ellipse>` elements so their line width stays even.
- **Spheres stay circles**, raised by their resting height on screen. A marble resting in a hole of radius r sits `√(R² − r²)` above the rim.
- **Frame and labels by silhouette.** A fixed fraction of the spacing put the bottom coordinate labels under the near pieces. Place each label a fixed gap beyond the silhouette of the piece it names: above, the higher of the face and the raised piece's top; below, the face plus the side showing under it. At tilt 1 this gives the same positions as before.
- **Labels beyond a board's edge** (DVONN's sand reaches past every piece): place each label a gap beyond the edge on screen along its direction, and let every label on one side reach as far as the farthest, so letters stay level and numbers stay in a column. Fit the frame's left and right separately to what lies on each side; fitted symmetrically it left about 17px of empty sand on one side of a phone. On phones with coordinates off, an unlabelled view without the labels' room lets the board grow (DVONN: 341 → 353px at 390 wide).

## Drawing order and interaction

- **Far first.** Sort spaces by screen y and draw the far rows first, so nearer pieces stand in front.
- **Passes.** Draw all bases, then the outline layer, then all raised pieces. With outlines in their own layer above the bases, a single pass drew a far space's outline across the raised piece in front of it. Passes are right only while a raised piece never overlaps a neighbouring base; work out from the geometry that it cannot at the chosen tilt before relying on it.
- **Clicks on raised pieces.** A raised piece reaches past its own space's face, onto the space behind. Give the piece the same click and hover handlers as its space (`role="presentation"`; the space keeps keyboard focus), and put the space's hit area on its base.
- **Static offsets on a wrapper.** A raised piece's lift is a static `transform`; put it on a wrapper around the node the animator tweens. GSAP reads a transform attribute on its target as its own x/y, so a `y` tween replaced the lift.

## Real pieces

- **Model the piece from photos of the published game,** and expect corrections in rounds. ZÈRTZ's rings, by round:
  1. Flat-topped cylinders, then cones with a flat top: the flat annulus with a rim line read as a seat for the marble.
  2. Plain cones sloping straight from the base to the hole's rim: the user pointed out the rim stands clear of the table.
  3. Thin hollow cones (height 0.155 × spacing, shell 0.045 × spacing, a darker gradient than the flat view's rings): through the hole, only the rim's cut edge shows on the far side, and below it the table in the cone's shadow.
- **A top with several hollows, as one height profile** (TZAAR: a flat edge, a groove, a raised ring, a sunken centre). Write `topProfile(distance)` and draw the top as bands between distances: the far half of every band first, edge inward, so a lower band further in shows where it is nearer you; then each near half, centre outward, so a higher band further out hides a lower one behind it. Shade each band by its slope on a vertical gradient, darken by depth (a hollow sits in the shade of the edges round it; without that the white pieces' groove barely showed), and keep specks to what `isSeen` says is visible, off steep slopes and off metal. The user's corrections, round 1: the sunken centre is filleted, not cut straight; the groove's walls slope straight to a flat floor; the outer side is straight up and down like GIPF's, only its corners rounded; seams as GIPF's and LYNGK's. Round 2: the centre was too big (0.48 R); measured on rulebook renders and a photo it is 0.35–0.38 R. Measure before resizing a feature. Pin the corrected profile in a unit test.
- **Making a lower shape thinner made it flatter.** When the user asks for a thinner piece, change the part they mean (ZÈRTZ: the shell) and keep the overall height.
- **Outline of a raised shape.** Before foreshortening, a shape's top and base are circles. Its outline is the hull of the two: their shared outer tangents, computed on the circles, then each point's y scaled by the tilt. When the top lies within the base, the base ellipse alone is the outline.
- **See-through holes.** Cut the hole out of the shape's path with `fill-rule: evenodd`, using the overlap of the hole's top and bottom openings, which is a lens between their crossing points. Draw the part of the top opening outside that lens as the far inner wall (a crescent), and tint the lens a little for the shadow under a hollow piece.
- **A piece sitting down in a hole** (YINSH's marker in its ring) is drawn between the holder and the holder's near half, with the holder's mark after both. That order is right only while the piece is in the hole: rising out of it or dropping into it, the piece must pass over the near half and the mark once its base clears the holder's top. Draw it twice with the same transition, one copy in the hole's place and one after the mark, each shown only on its side of that height (YINSH: `dropIn`/`liftOut` take a `layer`; the upper copy has opacity 0 at rest).

## Motion seen from the side

Separate every raised piece into a body and a shadow node, so the shadow stays on the board while the body moves.

- **Jumps** arc: the flier moves the ground track, and the body rises about 30 units over the piece it takes, with a slight swell (1.06) in place of the straight-down view's 1.28 swell toward the eye. The shadow slides along the ground track.
- **Landings** rebound along the jump's direction: past the space, back, forward, settled (2.4, −1.2, 0.45, 0 units; 0.07 s each, `sine.inOut`; about 3px past on a desktop screen). The user rejected a vertical bounce here; the rebound reads as the jump's momentum. The user tuned the overshoot down from 5px to 4px to 3px.
- **Pieces leaving the board** float straight up (about 34 units) as they fade, with almost no swell; the shadow shrinks and fades in place.
- **Placements** drop in, as in `animation-lessons.md`, followed by a small wobble (4 units, then 1.5).
- **Undo and history** keep the 0.18 s budget: a quick lift-and-fade for an undone placement, and no landing rebound.
- **Landing on a stack** (LYNGK) bounces mostly up and down, lower than a drop, with only a little carry along the hop: the body rises 2.5 units then 0.8, about 0.24 s in all, while the node carries 1 unit along the hop and back. The user found ZÈRTZ's sideways rebound too lateral for a stack landing on a stack.
- **Landing behind a nearer stack.** The flier is drawn above the board so it never passes under the stacks it hops over, which also put it in front of the nearer stack it lands behind. Over the flier, draw a `<use>` copy of every stack, each shown only while that stack's point is nearer the viewer than the flier's ground position, checked in an `onUpdate` every frame until it lands. Leave out the points the flier leaves, hovers over and lands on. Hide the copies with `display: none`, not `visibility`: a `<use>` copy hidden by `visibility` still paints any part of the piece that sets `visibility="visible"` itself, such as a turning marker's (YINSH: a marker flipping behind the ring showed over it).
- **A completed stack leaving** (LYNGK's scored stack) starts its float only after the landing bounce settles; otherwise the stack beneath rises while the top piece is still coming down, and reads as pieces passing through each other. Swap the stack landed on and the flier for one drawing of the whole stack as the float starts: two half-transparent stacks fading together show through each other. Undoing it drops the whole stack back in from above before the moved part hops home.

## Lathe-drawn discs (GIPF)

- **Draw a round piece as a lathe.** A profile gives the radius at each height up the disc (a straight side with rounded fillets top and bottom). Paint about 12 ellipse slices bottom first, each of the profile's radius at its height, so each leaves only its front edge showing. Fill each with a horizontal gradient chosen by which way that slice faces, from 9 shade levels; put the gradients in one `<defs>` per SVG under a prefix (`gipf-board`, `gipf-icon-{id}`), not per piece.
- **Model from a photo and expect corrections.** The user's GIPF corrections, in order: the centre is level with the edge, not sunken; a circular trough runs inset from the edge, and the underside ring nests in the trough below, so a GIPF-piece sits flush; the trough wider and further in, leaving a wider rim; edges less round; the crease between stacked discs comes mostly from a slight rise (the upper disc resting on its ring); pieces a little thicker. A trough seen at the tilt: its opening as an ellipse, the far outer wall as the crescent between the opening and the floor's edge (the opening lowered by the depth), the centre's near wall as a band.
- **Marks on top faces** sit at the top face's height in each piece's own group, after the piece and outside its animated node. **Arrows** lie on the board plane: lay them out in flat coordinates, then scale y by the tilt, and draw them above the pieces so tall stacks in front do not hide them. **Click areas** for a target under a piece: a transparent outline of the tallest piece that could stand there, above the pieces; give pieces with nothing to choose `pointer-events: none`, so clicks reach the dot or target behind. Where stacks of any height are targets (DVONN), each target is its own stack's outline, sorted far first so the nearer stack takes the overlap; probe each target's top face and near edge with `elementFromPoint` in a script and confirm every one hits itself.

## Stacked discs (LYNGK)

- **Discs rest on rings.** Each disc stands on its underside ring a gap above the disc below, and the bottom disc stands the same gap above the board, so the crease between discs and the line under every stack read as on the table. LYNGK and GIPF settled on a gap of 0.32 of a disc's thickness, with discs 0.2826 of the radius thick. Under each stack, add a contact shadow: a dark radial gradient filling the stack's footprint, which shows through the gap.
- **Keep the stack height when changing the gap.** When the user asks for a wider crease, thin the discs so a full stack keeps its height, and show both versions side by side: a taller stack covers more of the point behind it.
- **Marks on stacks** go in the stack's outer wrapper, after the stack, outside its animated body: the stack's own wrapper hides them with the stack when it lifts off. Symptom: the ring of a stack in flight hung in the air at its old point for a moment.

## Nested donuts (DVONN)

- **The user corrected the profile in four rounds:** not a lopsided valley, not one even arc, but a top that curves down from each edge to a flat floor in the middle (35% of the width flat, 0.3 of the thickness deep), with a straight outer side and only its corners rounded (0.22 of the thickness; the hole's lips 0.15, top and bottom). The underside bulges by the same depth, so pieces nest: a single piece stands on its bulge with its edge raised off the board (0.06 R), and a piece on another sits in its dish with a seam (0.05 R). Chosen: thickness 0.2 R, hole 0.34 R. The underside never shows from this angle, so only the stacking changes on screen. Show a cross-section of two nested pieces beside the drawing each round.
- **Choose the stack cap by what it hides.** Compute, from the outlines, the share of each piece behind a full stack still showing (the two in the row behind, and two rows back) and show the caps side by side with those shares; the user judged by them and chose 13. The row-behind share levels off (about 54% from a cap of 12 up), because the front stack covers the same lower part of those faces at any height; only the space two rows back gets buried as the cap rises. Past the cap the pieces between bottom and top thin to bands.
- **Speck every surface** at one density per unit of area: the face down to the hole's lip, the outer side, the far half of each hole wall (dimmed to 0.75, in shadow), and the thinned bands of a tall stack. The user asked for flecks on all surfaces after the hole walls and lips were left plain; sides had carried half the face's density.
- **The count in the hole**: phones set the floor. At 390 wide the board's units are about 0.49 px, so a count of 0.76 R (0.64 R from 10 up) is about 10.5px; the user chose that over 0.88 / 0.64 / 0.52, and may raise it after trials. Compare sizes on the real board by overriding the `font-size` attribute with injected CSS (`text.count[font-size^="24.8"]`), not by editing the code per option.
- **The catch-light** that parted black pieces on the flat view is unneeded once nested pieces have a seam; the user switched it off.

## Stacks past the cap (DVONN, TZAAR)

- **Settle, then shrink.** A move that takes a stack past its cap lands and bounces at the full combined height, as any landing does; once the bounce settles, the board takes over the whole stack at that height and shrinks it to the cap over 0.3 s (`power2.inOut`) as the pieces between bottom and top thin out. Symptom of the first version: the stack was thinned the moment the moving pieces touched down, cutting the landing off, which the user found jarring. Undo and history (0.18 s) still draw it thinned at once.
- **Animate the cap, for that stack only.** `stackDiscs` takes a fractional cap, so the animator tweens a number from `min(landedOn, cap) + moved` down to `cap` and draws the landing stack with `{ ...model, cap }`. Hand every other stack the board's own model object (`modelAt(key, model)` returns it unchanged), or each frame builds a new object per stack and redraws all of them. Start the takeover at the flier's resting height, so the swap from flier to board stack shows no jump.
- **Choose the cap by what stays visible behind, per orientation.** Compute the share of a single piece behind a stack left showing, by height, for the nearest spaces behind it on screen, and for the space two rows back once caps get high. TZAAR's columns put the nearest piece straight behind: 89% showing behind a 2, 44% behind a 5, nothing behind an 8; the user chose a cap of 5. Rotated a quarter turn (flat sides toward the players), the nearest pieces behind stand half a piece to either side: 68% behind a 5, 54% behind a 9. The user chose 9 there; 11 left only 22% of the piece two rows back. Show the shares beside screenshots of a scenario with singles behind stacks of every height.

## Hops over stacks (DVONN)

A stack hops in a straight line over any number of stacks, of any height, in one arc.

- **Clear each stack passed over at its own height**, not at the tallest's: held at the tallest height, the flier hung over a low stack and then dropped straight down.
- **Put the top of the arc where the ground track crosses the stack that sets the height**, clamped to the middle half of the hop, and come down only once past it. With the top fixed at half the hop's time, the flier kept climbing after it had passed a tall stack: a hitch, as if it jumped again.
- **Set off along the ground as soon as the footprints stay apart:** search for the smallest delay that keeps the flier's footprint clear of every stack until the body is above it. Waiting for the body to clear the tallest stack before moving read as a lag before the jump.
- **Occluders over the flier**: show a nearer stack's copy only while the flier is lower than that stack's top. Above it, no line of sight can pass through the stack, so the switch happens where nothing changes on screen; switched on when footprints stopped overlapping, a nearer stack painted over a flier still above it.
- **Cut-off stacks** float up 0.04 s after the landing, not after its bounce settles: waiting read as a 0.28 s pause. (LYNGK's completed stack waits because it includes the stack just landed on; DVONN's cut-offs are elsewhere.) A landing that takes a stack past the cap settles first: see *Stacks past the cap* below.
- **Undo of a move** (the user's choice, as GIPF's placements): the stack hops home and stays chosen; the next Undo puts it down. The session restages the selection in `beforeNewState` on `kind === 'undo'`.
